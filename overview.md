# M8 Learning Plan — Overview

9-week plan (+ optional Week 10) to rebuild fluency on the Dirtywave M8, ship two tracks, and close five specific gaps: instrument tables, sound design, arrangement, mixing, mastering.

## A note before you read

This plan was built for a producer who realized they had a drawer full of trackers, grooveboxes and samplers but no recent finished tracks. The diagnosis was simple: they kept buying new gear to spark ideas instead of going deeper into the tools that already worked. M8 was the one device that produced finished work in the past, so they built a plan to rebuild fluency, close their real gaps, and ship tracks again.

It's been generalized for sharing. The structure and the specific corrections are real. Take what's useful, ignore the rest.

## Why this plan exists

M8 is the only standalone device with a proven finishing track record. Re-entry friction is lower than learning anything new. Once M8 fluency is back, other gear becomes the next project.

## Five gaps being targeted

- **Instrument tables** — feel cryptic, blocked deeper use of M8
- **Sound design** — over-reliance on presets, no personal sonic identity
- **Arrangement** — tracks meander, don't "arrive"
- **Mixing** — elements fight, mix doesn't translate across systems
- **Mastering / finalization** — raw renders, no polish, can't share confidently

The first three are connected: they're all *shaping how sound changes over time* at different scales — milliseconds (envelopes), bars (tables/automation), minutes (arrangement). Same skill, three views.

Mixing is the fourth axis: *shaping how sounds relate to each other in frequency and space*. Mastering is the final polish that lets the result translate.

## Operating principles

- **Two projects, two finishes.** Project 1 (Weeks 1–6) uses presets and lets you focus on technique. Project 2 (Weeks 7–9) uses your own instruments and applies everything.
- **Render every week, even when bad.** No "I'll polish later." Bounce, save, move on.
- **Three threads per week:** M8 technique + synthesis fundamental + arrangement principle (Phase 1). Mix and finalization replace technique threads in Phase 2 onwards.
- **Cumulative, not replacement.** Each week's deliverable builds on the previous week's project.
- **Stuck? Copy.** Reverse-engineer patches from Dirtywave Discord / YouTube. Faster than from-scratch.
- **No new gear, no comparisons.** M8 only for the duration. Optional Week 10 brings Ableton in for post-production only.

## Cadence

2–3 focused sessions per week, ~60 min each. ~20 hours total over Weeks 1–9. Optional Week 10 adds ~3 hours.

**Realistic timing expectation:**
- ~6 weeks to first release-ready track (Project 1)
- ~3 weeks for Project 2 after the library is built
- After the plan ends: 4–5 weeks per release-ready track is sustainable cadence. Some tracks faster, some slower. The number that matters is "finished," not "every X weeks."

## Anti-burnout rules

- Miss a week → **don't catch up.** Continue from the calendar. Catching up kills plans.
- Stale week → do it dirty and ugly. Bad output > no output.
- No comparing output to YouTube tracks. Reference for *learning*, not *judging*.
- After Week 9 → **deliberate week off.** Active rest. No M8, no other gear, no browsing.

## Weekly structure

Three phases. Each week is an epic with granular tasks and a clear deliverable.

**Phase 1 — Learn (Weeks 1–4):** pure technique, no finishing pressure
- Week 1 — Re-entry
- Week 2 — LFO to filter + envelopes
- Week 3 — Pitch slides, Tracking, filters
- Week 4 — Retriggers, arpeggios, LFO concept

**Phase 2 — Apply (Weeks 5–7):** Project 1 finish + library
- Week 5 — Mix Project 1 (dedicated mix week, no new technique)
- Week 6 — Finalize and ship Project 1
- Week 7 — Starter instrument library (no track, recovery week)

**Phase 3 — Polish (Weeks 8–9):** Project 2
- Week 8 — Build Project 2 (own instruments, all techniques)
- Week 9 — Finalize and ship Project 2 (Scope A)

**Optional Week 10:** Scope B Ableton mix + master pass on Project 2.

**Plus three standalone reference pages:**
- [Mixing Reference](reference/mixing.md) — used Week 5 onwards for balancing tracks
- [Finalization Reference](reference/finalization.md) — used Week 6, 9, 10 for mastering after mixing
- [Generative Toolkit Reference](reference/generative.md) — six generative techniques (LFO shapes, phrase randomization, table TIC modes, Hypersynth, table-as-arpeggiator) woven into Weeks 2, 4, 7, 8

## Mixing and mastering

Finishing a track means making it sound finished, not just rendering raw. Two stages, two reference pages, separate weeks:

### Mixing (balance the elements against each other)
- **Scope A (on-device M8 only)**: default. Mixer View, per-instrument EQ, send levels, TRIG-based side-chain. Used Week 5 (Project 1), Week 8 (Project 2).
- **Scope B (render stems to Ableton)**: optional. Used Week 10 only.

### Mastering / finalization (polish the mixed track)
- **Scope A (on-device M8 only)**: default. Built-in EQ, OTT, limiter, soft-clip via Mixer and Limiter & Mix Scope views. Used Week 6 (Project 1), Week 9 (Project 2).
- **Scope B (optional Ableton pass)**: optional refinement — metering, finer EQ, deliberate loudness targeting. Used Week 10 only.

**You can release a track straight off the M8.** The render is a 44.1kHz stereo WAV, which is the format distributors and Bandcamp/Soundcloud accept, and the main bus has a real master chain. The one thing the M8 lacks is LUFS metering — and since streaming platforms normalize loudness on playback anyway, that matters less than it sounds. Both Project 1 and Project 2 ship as releasable files. See [Finalization Reference → Releasing a Scope A render](reference/finalization.md#releasing-a-scope-a-render).

**Always mix before you master.** Mastering can't fix a bad mix. Both stages are time-boxed: 60 min max per session.

## What this plan deliberately does NOT cover

- MIDI out to external gear
- Sampling deep-dives
- Mixing/mastering perfectionism (render at -6dB peak, done)
- Reading the manual cover-to-cover (already did, didn't help)
